FFB Tuning Checklist

For iRacing on a direct drive base

Work top to bottom and change one thing at a time. Most bad force feedback comes from doing these steps out of order: chasing strength in two places at once, or adding effects before the base signal is clean.

1. The rig

  • The base is bolted to a rigid mount. If the wheel deck flexes when you turn hard, fix that first: flex eats fine detail before any setting can.
  • Base firmware and its software (Pit House on MOZA) are up to date, and so is iRacing.
  • The wheel is calibrated in iRacing with the same rotation you set in the base software, so the on-screen wheel matches your hands.

2. The wheelbase software

  • Pick one place for the strength decision. A common approach: run the base near its full output and do the scaling in iRacing, because iRacing's setting is per car.
  • Turn off, or keep low, any effect that adds force on top of the sim: extra road texture, kerb effects and the like. Each one spends headroom the steering signal needs.
  • Start with damping, friction, inertia and smoothing low. Add them only to fix a specific problem, such as oscillation on the straights or a notchy centre.

3. iRacing

  • Know which way the main force setting works: it is the force iRacing maps to 100% of your wheel. A higher value gives a lighter wheel and less clipping. A lower value gives a heavier wheel and more clipping.
  • Drive two or three clean laps, then use the Auto button so iRacing sets strength from that car's real peak. Repeat it for every car you race.
  • On a direct drive base, leave minimum force at zero. It exists to push weak gear and belt wheels past their dead zone.
  • Keep iRacing's damping low; the same trade-off as in the base software applies.

4. The clipping check on track

  • Show the force feedback meter in the black box and watch it for a full lap, not a straight.
  • A little red on the biggest kerb strike of the lap is fine. Red through the middle of medium-speed corners means you are clipping: raise iRacing's force value and retest.
  • In your hardest braking zone, notice whether the wheel goes quiet or dead at peak brake pressure. If it does, you are clipping there too.
  • Test every change on the same car and track, for at least three laps, before you judge it.
  • When it feels right, save the profile and note the values per car.
