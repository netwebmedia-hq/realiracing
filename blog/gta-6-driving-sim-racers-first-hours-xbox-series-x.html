What I drove

In these hours I drove several kinds of vehicle. There were pickup trucks, a red retro sports car, a dark retro sports coupe, a motorcycle and a grey SUV. At one point I took a car off a tow truck. The world around them looks like a sunny coastal region, with palm trees, highways, long bridges over water and run-down streets.

I did not set out to test cars. Much of the driving happened with a police wanted level on, so these are chase moments, not calm laps. Keep that in mind when you watch.

A sim racer on a controller

I am used to a wheel, pedals and force feedback. Here I had a controller and a game that does not try to be a simulator. I will not give handling numbers or a verdict, because I did not measure anything. What I can say is what the footage shows: cars that slide sideways, cut across grass and hit gates and poles. A proper handling breakdown needs more hours than this.

If you race in iRacing, you will see how different this is. In iRacing a big slide usually means I have made a mistake. In the gas station clip I slid sideways and kept driving.

How I streamed GTA 6 from Chile

Here is the setup. I do not use a capture card. The Xbox picture reaches OBS through Xbox Remote Play in a browser window on the PC. OBS captures that window and streams 1080p60 to YouTube. I use a Logitech C920 webcam for the facecam and a USB audio interface for the microphone.

It worked, but it has real downsides that I saw during the stream:

  • Remote Play pauses when its window loses focus, so clicking another window can freeze the picture.
  • It disconnects after a long idle. A disconnect near the end of my session ended the feed.
  • There is picture delay if you play from the PC screen.

The better way to run this is to play on the TV that is wired to the Xbox, and use the PC only as a mirror for OBS. The cleaner fix is a capture card, which takes the Remote Play window out of the chain. If you plan to stream a console, that is the route to look at.
