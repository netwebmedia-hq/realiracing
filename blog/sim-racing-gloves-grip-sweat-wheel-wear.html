Plenty of drivers race on a MOZA R9 without gloves and think they're fine, until a full endurance race has their hands sliding off the wheel during trail braking into Eau Rouge at Spa. Grip isn't just about comfort; it's about control, consistency, and protecting the hardware you're spending real money on. Sim racing gloves aren't luxury padding. They're a functional input device that changes how you feel feedback and how your rig feels your inputs.

The weird truth is that most sim racers don't talk about gloves seriously. We obsess over wheelbase firmware, pedal calibration curves, and FFB clipping, but we ignore the interface between our hands and the wheel—the one thing that actually touches the hardware every single session. Sweat, pressure, texture, and even finger temperature all affect how much grip you have and how clearly you can sense what the MOZA R9 is telling you through the rim. I'll walk through what matters, why it matters, and how to choose gloves that actually work for your rig.
