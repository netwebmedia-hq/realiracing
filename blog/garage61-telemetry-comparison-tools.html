Garage61 vs Telemetry Comparison Tools: Which Fits Your iRacing Workflow

I've spent enough hours staring at lap data that I can spot a brake-pressure inconsistency in a raw CSV file. But the tool you use to find those inconsistencies matters far more than most drivers realize. Some telemetry platforms excel at real-time dashboards during a race weekend; others shine when you're dissecting a single lap against a baseline two weeks later. Understanding what each tool is built for—and what it won't do—saves you time and money.

What Garage61 Does Well

Garage61 is purpose-built for iRacing setup development. It pulls telemetry directly from iRacing replays, and its interface focuses on the metrics that matter most for tuning: brake pressure curves, throttle application, pedal timing, suspension loads, and tire temperature. I appreciate that it doesn't try to be everything—it's lean, it's fast, and the data export is clean.

The heat-map overlay feature is genuinely useful. You can visualize where on the track you're braking harder or carrying more speed, and compare that visually against your own baseline or a friend's lap. That spatial awareness often reveals patterns that a table of numbers won't. On my MOZA R9 rig, I can load a session, spot a corner where my brake consistency is drifting, and then go back out and focus.

  • Direct iRacing replay import (no manual data export needed)
  • Brake pressure and pedal-input comparison overlays
  • Tire-pressure and temperature tracking per lap
  • Setup diff tool to highlight suspension and aerodynamic changes
  • Works offline once telemetry is loaded
When to Choose Garage61

Garage61's strength is surgical precision on single-lap analysis. If you're chasing consistency in your brake point or studying how suspension stiffness affects your turn-in, it's hard to beat.

Telemetry Comparison Alternatives and Trade-offs

iSpeed is the most common alternative I see discussed in sim racing forums. It imports iRacing telemetry and builds a full replay viewer—you can watch your lap onscreen, synced to data overlays, which is powerful for spotting where a corner went wrong. The dashboard is more polished and has a steeper learning curve, but once you're comfortable, multi-session comparisons are faster.

Motec i2 is another option, though it's overkill for most iRacers and has a larger price tag. It's the tool professional racing teams use, and it's built for real-world data from CAN buses and data loggers. You can import iRacing telemetry into it, but you're paying for features you'll never touch. That said, if you're already familiar with Motec from real racing or other sims, it's a seamless workflow.

On the lighter end, some drivers just export iRacing CSVs and analyze them in Excel or Google Sheets. It's tedious, but it's free and teaches you to think about what the numbers mean. I did this early on—no regrets, because it forced me to understand correlation rather than just chase a red line on a graph.

  • iSpeed: Replay sync, multi-session stacking, real-time dashboard, steeper UI learning curve, cloud storage optional
  • Motec i2: Industry-standard analysis, supports dozens of data formats, expensive, built for professional teams
  • Excel/CSV: Free, teaches fundamentals, time-consuming, no built-in visualization
  • Garage61: Fast, iRacing-native, offline-capable, narrower feature scope

My Workflow: How I Use Garage61 in Practice

Here's how I approach a practice session on a new track. I'll run 10-15 laps, export the best one, and load it into Garage61 to check my brake consistency and throttle smoothness. I'm looking for whether my inputs are repeatable, not whether I'm a tenth faster than last week. Consistency first; speed follows.

Then I'll load a reference lap from a faster driver or a setup shop baseline, overlay it in Garage61, and scan for the obvious gaps. Usually I find one or two corners where my pedal work is sloppy—brake release timing, or early throttle. That focus beats trying to tune suspension by feel when the real problem is driver input.

I don't use Garage61's setup-diff feature as much as I should, but when I'm A/B testing something on my MOZA CRP2 load-cell pedals, like a pressure curve change, the ability to compare lap telemetry side-by-side is invaluable. It removes guesswork.

Which Tool Should You Pick?

Start with this question: do you want to watch your lap replay synced to data, or just analyze numbers? If you want the replay—you want to see the corner and the telemetry at the same time—iSpeed is the stronger choice. If you want speed and simplicity, Garage61 wins.

Your budget matters. Garage61 has a one-time license cost (~$50-70, though check current pricing); iSpeed is subscription-based. If you're on a tight budget and don't care about replay sync, Garage61 is cheaper long-term. If you race frequently and want cloud-synced data across devices, iSpeed's subscription might feel worth it.

Your learning stage also factors in. If you're still figuring out what telemetry means, Excel or a free CSV viewer teaches you more than a polished tool will. Once you know what to look for, a dedicated tool saves hours. If you're serious about climbing iRating, Garage61 or iSpeed will accelerate that journey compared to manual analysis.

Test Before You Buy

Try both if possible. Many telemetry tools offer free trials or demos. Spend an hour with Garage61, an hour with iSpeed, and decide which interface clicks for you. You'll use whichever tool more if it doesn't frustrate you.

Integration With Your Setup Workflow

The best telemetry tool is the one you'll actually open after every session. If Garage61's interface feels natural to you, you'll use it consistently and build real understanding. If iSpeed's replay sync solves a problem you actually have, the subscription pays for itself in faster learning.

On my rig, I run Garage61 between sessions while I'm reviewing notes or tweaking my MOZA R9 force-feedback settings. It's quick enough that I don't get distracted. I load a replay, check brake consistency, maybe compare against a baseline, and I'm back in iRacing within five minutes. That workflow suits me.

Consider also whether you'll share data with others. If you're in a league where the fast guys share reference laps, Garage61's file format is easier to work with than proprietary cloud storage. If your coaching team uses iSpeed, you'll want to match them so the workflow is seamless.

FAQ

Can I use both Garage61 and iSpeed at the same time?

Yes, absolutely. Many drivers use Garage61 for quick session-to-session analysis and iSpeed for deeper replay study. There's no conflict. You just export telemetry to both tools and use each where it's strongest.

Does Garage61 work with other sims besides iRacing?

Garage61 is built specifically for iRacing. If you race other sims like Assetto Corsa Competizione or rFactor 2, you'll need a different tool or rely on those games' native telemetry viewers.

Is learning to read telemetry worth the time investment?

Yes, strongly. Understanding your own telemetry cuts the trial-and-error on setups and driver inputs. Most drivers who plateau at a certain iRating do so because they can't see their own inconsistencies. Telemetry tools make those visible. Plan on 2-3 hours to get comfortable, then the ROI is huge.

Can I use a telemetry tool to fix my braking without a load-cell pedal?

Telemetry will show you what your inputs are, but without load-cell feedback, you'll struggle to reproduce consistent brake pressure. If you're on a spring-based or hydraulic friction pedal setup, telemetry helps you spot problems—but a tool like the MOZA CRP2 load cells makes the fix much easier to feel and train.
