Sim Racing Black Friday 2026: What to Wait For

Planning around Black Friday is hard because you do not know what any item will cost on the day. It falls on November 27 this year, and until brands announce something, every deal you see is a rumour, a countdown banner or a recycled number from last year.

This post is a way to plan a sim racing purchase around that gap. I race iRacing on a MOZA R9 (~9 N·m) with MOZA pedals, but the logic below draws on published specs and common setup practice, not a hands-on test of every product. Load-cell pedals such as the CRP2 come up again below. This post will not tell you what will be on sale, because nobody can know that yet. It will tell you what is worth waiting for, what is not, and how to avoid buying the wrong thing in a hurry.

Decide the order before the sales start

The sales calendar pushes you toward the item with the biggest discount banner. A better filter is which part of your rig is currently limiting your driving. For most people building on a budget, the answer is the pedals or the seat, long before it is the wheelbase.

A load-cell brake reads pressure instead of travel, which is why it makes braking repeatable from lap to lap. If you are still on potentiometer pedals, I would put that upgrade ahead of a stronger wheelbase. I covered the options in the budget load-cell pedals guide, and the logic there still holds.

A wheelbase upgrade only makes sense if you already know what is missing from the one you have. If you are weighing a step up, the torque tiers explainer and the R9 versus R12 comparison lay out where the extra force shows up in practice. The R12 is a ~12 N·m base, and the jump from ~9 N·m is smaller than the number suggests unless your rig is rigid enough to use it.

What I would wait for

Some categories get discounted often enough that waiting costs you little. Others rarely move. Without announced deals I cannot say which will drop this year, so I sort items by how replaceable they are rather than by promised savings.

  • Pedals and shifters: several brands run promotions on peripherals, and the ecosystem matters more than the single part, so check what plugs into the base you already own.
  • Monitors: a 1440p panel at ~120 Hz is a common target for sim racing, and displays are often part of seasonal promotions, though I cannot say which models.
  • Cockpit frames and seats: bulky and heavy to ship, so promotions can be tempting. Check delivery cost and weight limits before you get excited.
  • Accessories such as gloves, cable management and button boxes: low stakes, so a missed sale costs almost nothing.

What I would not wait for

If your current gear is failing or your racing time is constrained, waiting is its own cost. A pedal set with a worn sensor or a seat that gives you back pain is worth fixing now, because every week of bad habits is a week of practice in the wrong direction. I wrote about the posture side in the fixed cockpit back pain guide.

I also would not wait for a product that is brand new or about to be replaced. Retailers often discount old stock when a successor appears, but the timing is unpredictable, and a new release rarely gets a deep cut in its first weeks. If you want the new model, price it as a full-cost purchase and treat any discount as a bonus.

A used wheelbase is a different route altogether. It can be a sound way to step up if you inspect it carefully, and the used wheelbase buying guide lists what to check before sending money.

How to read a deal when it shows up

A percentage tells you very little. A product listed at a high anchor price and then cut by a large percentage can still cost more than the same item did a month earlier. The only comparison that matters is today's price against the lowest price you can verify over the past few months, using a price-history tool or your own notes.

This is why you should start tracking now. Write down the price of each item on your list once a week, from the same store, in the same currency, and note shipping and any import cost on top. By the time November 27 arrives, you will know what a normal week looks like, and a real discount is easy to recognise.

Bundles deserve extra suspicion. A bundle can be a bargain when every part is something you wanted, and it can be a way to move a slow-selling accessory when one part is filler. Price the parts you actually need separately and compare.

Write the list down with two columns: what you need this season and what you would only like to have. Anything in the second column waits for a price you have already seen. Anything in the first column gets bought when a fair price appears, even if the sale is modest, because the point of the purchase is to race better, and a week of racing on the right part is worth more than a small extra saving on a wrong one. It also keeps you from buying a third accessory because the banner said it was the last day.

Compatibility and returns before checkout

Sim racing hardware lives in ecosystems. Parts from one brand, such as a MOZA base, pedals and rims, usually pair through the same software, and mixing brands usually works but adds a step. Before you buy, confirm that the new part connects to the base you own, that your PC has the ports and mounting holes it needs, and that the cockpit you have takes the bracket for it.

Then read the return policy of the specific retailer, not the brand. Sales periods sometimes carry their own return rules, and I have no way to tell you this year's terms in advance. Take a screenshot of the policy on the day you order, along with the order confirmation, so you have both if something arrives damaged or incompatible.

For gifts, the same rules apply with an added deadline: delivery before the date matters more than saving a little, and my 2026 gift guide is built around that constraint.

A plan for this November

Do not buy the day the first email arrives. Check your tracked list on the Friday itself, again on Monday, and decide with the numbers in front of you. If an item on your list has not reached a price you have seen before, put it back on the list for the next sale or a regular week.

One practical point about cockpits. A fixed aluminium cockpit often handles upgrades well because the mounting rails take most brackets without drilling, so a new pedal plate or wheel mount can be an evening of work. If you are buying into a frame for the first time, it is worth reading the assembly guide before choosing, because assembly time and flex change how much a stronger wheelbase will give you.

Once brands publish real offers, update your list. Until then, the most productive things to do with your time are tracking prices, deciding which part limits you, and checking compatibility. Those three steps cost nothing and work whether or not a good deal appears.

FAQ

When is Black Friday in 2026?

Black Friday falls on November 27, 2026. Many retailers start promotions earlier in the week or the weeks before, and some run through the following Monday, so the exact window depends on the store.

Should I wait for Black Friday to buy a sim racing wheel?

(FAQ) It depends on how much your current wheel limits you. If it works and you are saving for an upgrade, waiting a few weeks costs little. If it is failing or you cannot race comfortably, buying now is usually the better choice.

Which upgrade should come first, pedals or wheelbase?

For most people the pedals. A load-cell brake reads pressure, which makes braking more repeatable, while a stronger wheelbase mainly adds force and detail. If your pedals already use load cells, then a wheelbase or a more rigid cockpit becomes the next step.

How do I know if a Black Friday discount is real?

Compare the sale price with the lowest price you can verify over the previous months, not with the crossed-out list price. Track the item weekly from the same retailer before the sale, include shipping and import costs, and check the return policy that applies to the sale period.
